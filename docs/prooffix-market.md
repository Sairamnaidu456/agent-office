# ProofFix market and distribution report

Research date: 2026-10-04. Read-only external research; no accounts, posts, or customer messages created. Prices below are experiment choices, not established willingness to pay.

## Decision

Proceed only with the bounded technical pilot. Target solo developers whose coding agent says a browser bug is fixed. The proposed promise is: run the same regression against before/after app versions and retain inspectable evidence. A frozen declarative plan is a verification substrate; it is not itself autonomous bug discovery or AI test generation. Describe the present pilot accurately.

No evidence establishes that this wedge is unavailable in free tooling or commands a price. Playwright already has planner/generator/healer agents; local browser execution and trace/video artifacts are ordinary Playwright capabilities. Our potential advantage is packaging: fewer setup steps, preserved assertions, before/after commit identity, understandable results. Benchmark that convenience against plain Playwright before expanding.

## Official competitor evidence

- https://playwright.dev/docs/test-agents — planner explores applications, generator produces executable tests, healer repairs failing tests. A generic AI test generator competes directly with this free workflow.
- https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp — official browser tools for coding agents. Browser access is not a moat.
- https://www.qawolf.com/changelog/automation-ai — prompted/feature-driven deterministic Playwright generation. Vendor claims are not independently verified here.
- https://jam.dev/resolve — recordings/logs for coding agents plus CLI screenshots/videos for fix verification. Bug-to-agent handoff and evidence are already commercial product features.
- https://www.checklyhq.com/ — AI-enabled tests and reliability tools. Broad hosted monitoring is outside the pilot.

## Free versus paid offer to test

Free: local single-bug verification, example broken/fixed application, readable test/plan, result report, and clear unsupported-case handling. Do not charge merely to learn that the browser cannot reproduce the issue.

Potential paid package: $49 one-time founding license for packaged repository integration, reusable workflows, batch comparisons, branded/shareable HTML reports, and versioned support documentation. These features must exist before accepting money. User supplies model access when AI generation is added; clearly distinguish model costs from license costs. This price is a hypothesis, and a simple script may be a better outcome than a company.

At $49, 21 sales yield $1,029 gross. Under Gumroad's listed direct-sale fee of 10% plus $0.50 per transaction, 23 sales yield $1,127 gross and approximately $1,002.80 after that listed fee alone. Refunds, taxes, currency conversion, other applicable costs, and payout settlement can change actual receipt; do not report revenue from this arithmetic. There is no credible revenue deadline yet.

## Proposed measurable gates (internal experiment thresholds)

Technical: ten seeded reproducible browser bugs plus ten fixed versions. The unchanged plan must distinguish every selected broken/fixed pair with three repeated runs; any false verified-fix blocks release until explained and corrected. Report unsupported/authentication cases rather than silently skip. Measure setup time and compare the same cases using standard Playwright.

Convenience: at least five independent target users run the pilot on their own local applications; at least three finish without agent-team intervention and at least three say the saved effort is preferable to their existing free workflow. These are proposed thresholds, not users already acquired.

Commercial: after 20 qualified users successfully exercise the product and see the concrete paid offer, require three actual paid purchases or stop paid-feature expansion and revise. A failed payment setup invalidates this commercial experiment; record purchase intent separately and never count it as revenue. No traffic means distribution is unresolved, not that demand is disproven.

Stop immediately if model usage needs unapproved expenditure, budget guard blocks work, or reliable verification requires a hosted service we cannot fund. Do not scale from likes, installs, or newsletter signups alone.

## Three specific distribution candidates and rules to check

1. Show HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html and https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html . Official guidance requires something people can try, discourages signup barriers, excludes landing pages, and requires a substantive personal project with discussion participation. An agent-generated one-off is a poor fit; use only after the pilot is substantial and transparently explain how it was built. Do not impersonate a human or solicit votes.
2. Product Hunt: https://www.producthunt.com/launch and https://www.producthunt.com/launch/before-launch . Official guide says usage is free, company accounts are prohibited, and new accounts must wait one week before posting. Requires an appropriate authorized personal maker account; do not create a fabricated person. Launch a usable product, not a promise; check current rules again at submission.
3. r/SideProject: https://www.reddit.com/r/SideProject/ and https://www.reddit.com/r/SideProject/about/rules . The rules page returned only a community-rules shell in this research, so permission for promotional/agent-authored posts has NOT been verified. Inspect actual rules and platform account requirements before any post. This is a candidate channel, not a posting authorization.

These channels can reach developers but are not exclusively solo AI developers. No guaranteed impressions, conversions, or permission to mass-message follows from their availability.
